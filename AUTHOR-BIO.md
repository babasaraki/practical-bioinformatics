# Author biography — the four versions a book needs

Rewritten from the CV (`Curriculum Vitae_UNENGLAND_Umar Ahmad`, 5 pp.) and the
affiliations list supplied with it. The version that shipped in the first build was
built from an older profile and was wrong in three ways that matter: it gave the post
at Sa'adu Zungur University as current, described Africa CDC as a role held in the
past, and omitted BioSeq, AGARI, the professorship and the ORCID entirely.

A book does not have one biography. It has four, each written to a different length
for a different slot, and publishers ask for them separately. All four below state the
same facts in the same order — current posts, research, continental service, teaching,
open science — so a reader who meets two of them sees one consistent person.

House conventions followed here:

- **Third person throughout.** Chapman & Hall/CRC, Springer and Wiley all set author
  biographies in the third person.
- **Name and degree, not a title.** CRC house style opens `Umar Ahmad, PhD,` rather
  than `Dr Umar Ahmad`. The degree is given once, at first mention.
- **Present tense for current roles, past tense for former ones.** Three posts are
  concurrent and all three are current; the biography says so rather than implying a
  sequence.
- **Affiliations spelled out in full on first use**, with city and country.
- **No contact details or social handles.** An ORCID is the exception: it is an
  identifier, not a contact, and CRC will print one if supplied.
- **No unverifiable superlatives.** "Leading", "world-renowned" and "pioneering" read
  as padding to an acquisitions editor.

## Photograph

Needed for two slots, not three. The **book's interior** does not need one: CRC sets
the About the author page as text, and most of their monographs carry no photograph
inside. The **back cover** usually does, and that is the publisher's layout decision —
supply one and let them choose. **Amazon Author Central** needs one in practice; a
profile without a photograph looks abandoned.

Specification for both: 300 dpi or better, at least 1,200 px on the short edge, head
and shoulders, plain or softly defocused background, even lighting, no conference
lanyard. You must own the rights or hold a written release from the photographer —
CRC's permissions check asks for this explicitly.
